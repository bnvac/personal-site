A tour of the bench

Every screen below is the real app in dark mode, running on an example workshop.

Questions it answers

The things I actually ask my bench, and where the answer comes from.

"What can I build tonight?"

The idea board sorts parts into roles and only suggests builds it can name every part for, then says how to case it with the tools you own.

ideas · parts · workshop
"Will this filament print on that printer?"

Each printer's hotend, bed, nozzle and enclosure decide what it can load: load it, with care, or not as it stands with the reasons.

printers
"Reprint that bracket."

Sliced G-code is kept against its part and sent straight to Klipper or OctoPrint. The case finder measures STLs before a six-hour print.

printers · cases
"Which ESP32 is in the greenhouse?"

Firmware records which board runs what, keeps the image and its SHA-256, and gives the exact command to flash it back.

firmware
"Is the homelab up, and what's it costing?"

Machines are checked over TCP without root, and their draw adds up to watts and kWh a year, skipping any with no figure.

machines
"Do I already own one?"

Paste a shop link and the part fills itself in. QR labels on every drawer open the part on your phone with count buttons under your thumb.

parts · labels · prices

Hardware startups

Price history per shop and real delivery times for planning builds.

Home labs

Machines, images, checksums and the power bill, next to the parts.

Small on purpose

One Go binary, one SQLite file, one folder of photos. No database server, no npm install, no config. Upgrading is replacing the binary.

Written rather than imported: a KiCad footprint parser and renderer, an STL parser with a z-buffer rasteriser, and a paste-a-list parser for tools and plans.

An AI model is optional (Ollama, OpenAI-compatible or Anthropic), and anything it suggests is checked against the real inventory before you see it.
