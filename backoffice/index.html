Everything on the bench, connected

The real app in dark mode, running on an example workshop.

Parts

Every part, with a photo and a place.

Paste a product link from Adafruit, Pimoroni or The Pi Hut and the photo, price and part number fill in. Filters stack by drawer, maker, interface and tag, so “I2C sensors in drawer B” is two clicks.

One part

Stock, prices, and what's already spoken for.

Counts go up and down from the page, or from your phone by scanning the drawer's QR label. Parts reserved by a project show as committed, and every shop's price sits beside its real delivery time.

Projects

A parts list that knows what you're missing.

Import a BOM from KiCad or EasyEDA and it matches each line against your drawers. Marking a project building reserves its parts, and the pin budget checks the board has enough legs.

Wiring

Record the pins. It draws the wires.

Colours follow bench convention, red power and black ground, so the picture and the wire in your hand agree. Shared buses are worked out for you.

Idea board

What you can build tonight, from what you own.

It sorts your parts into roles and only suggests builds it can name every part for, then says how to box it based on your tools: print a case, buy a project box, or find one made for the board.

Printers

What each printer can actually load.

Record a printer properly once: hotend, bed, nozzle, enclosure, feed. Every filament comes back as load it, with care, or not as it stands with the reasons. Sliced G-code goes straight to Klipper or OctoPrint.

Firmware

Which board is running what.

The shelf says six ESP32s; this says the one in the greenhouse runs logger 1.4.0. The image and its checksum are kept with the exact command to flash it back.

Machines

The homelab is a shelf that's switched on.

Address, OS, install image and checksum for every box, a live up/down check without root, and what the lot costs to run in watts and kWh a year.

Workshop

Your tools, and what they add up to.

Paste the list you already have. It works out what you can make with it, like print size and through-hole soldering, and the idea board uses that to decide how each build gets made.

Built for anyone who shares a shelf

A group, a few machines, and nobody remembers which drawer the last one went in.

School makerspaces

Named logins with an activity log, so you know who took the last servo.

Robotics teams

Projects reserve their parts; BOMs import from KiCad and EasyEDA.

Community labs

One page that tells visitors what each printer can print.

Repair cafés

Firmware and flash commands kept with each board, so fixes repeat.

Hardware startups

Price history per shop and real delivery times for planning builds.

Home labs

Machines, images, checksums and the power bill, next to the parts.

Small on purpose

One Go binary, one SQLite file, one folder of photos. No database server, no npm install, no config. Upgrading is replacing the binary.

Written rather than imported: a KiCad footprint parser and renderer, an STL parser with a z-buffer rasteriser, and a paste-a-list parser for tools and plans.

An AI model is optional (Ollama, OpenAI-compatible or Anthropic), and anything it suggests is checked against the real inventory before you see it.
