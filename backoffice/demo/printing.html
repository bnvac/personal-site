What you can load

Across every machine you own. Press one for the long answer.

  • PLA Load it

    Stiff, dimensionally honest, and it creeps and softens in a hot car. Fine for anything living indoors.

    On your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro.

  • PETG With care

    Tougher than PLA and survives a sunny windowsill. It sticks to smooth PEI hard enough to take the sheet with it, so use a release agent or a textured plate.

    On your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro. prints better dry — stringing and a rough surface usually mean a damp spool

  • TPU 95A flexible With care

    Slow it right down. Softer shores than 95A are a direct-drive-only proposition.

    On your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro. prints better dry — stringing and a rough surface usually mean a damp spool

  • ABS Not as it stands

    Lifts off the bed and splits between layers in a draught. Small parts can get away with it; a tall one will not.

    Closest is your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, but it warps off an open bed — it needs still, warm air around it.

    • an enclosure £0–150a cardboard box or a grow tent over the printer does most of the job; the point is no draught
  • ASA Not as it stands

    ABS that does not go chalky in sunlight. The one to use for anything living outdoors.

    Closest is your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, but it warps off an open bed — it needs still, warm air around it.

    • an enclosure £0–150a cardboard box or a grow tent over the printer does most of the job; the point is no draught
  • Polypropylene PP Not as it stands

    Almost nothing sticks to it, including your bed. Printed on packing tape of the same plastic.

    Closest is your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, but it warps off an open bed — it needs still, warm air around it.

    • an enclosure £0–150a cardboard box or a grow tent over the printer does most of the job; the point is no draught
  • Nylon PA6, PA12 Not as it stands

    Drinks water out of the air in hours. Wet nylon spits, foams and comes out weak — drying is not optional, it is the whole job.

    Closest is your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, but it warps off an open bed — it needs still, warm air around it, and it has to go on dry, and it is wet within a day of opening the bag.

    • an enclosure £0–150a cardboard box or a grow tent over the printer does most of the job; the point is no draught
    • a 0.6 mm nozzle £5–15wider channel, fewer jams, and the extra width barely shows on a functional part
    • a filament dryer £30–90a food dehydrator with the trays out does the same job for less
  • Nylon CF PAHT-CF, PA-GF Not as it stands

    The chopped fibre is what makes it stiff and what destroys a brass nozzle. Short fibres also bridge a small nozzle and clog it, which is why 0.6 is the floor.

    Closest is your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, but it warps off an open bed — it needs still, warm air around it, and it the fibre in it will eat a brass nozzle inside a spool, and it needs a 0.6 mm nozzle or bigger; this one is 0.4 mm, and it has to go on dry, and it is wet within a day of opening the bag.

    • an enclosure £0–150a cardboard box or a grow tent over the printer does most of the job; the point is no draught
    • a 0.6 mm hardened steel nozzle £8–20brass wears open as you print, so the holes creep wider and the part stops matching the model; and anything narrower bridges across the opening and blocks the melt zone
    • a filament dryer £30–90a food dehydrator with the trays out does the same job for less
  • Polycarbonate PC Not as it stands

    Genuinely tough and stays stiff when hot. Warps harder than ABS and wants every degree you can give it.

    Closest is your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, but it warps off an open bed — it needs still, warm air around it, and it has to go on dry, and it is wet within a day of opening the bag.

    • an enclosure £0–150a cardboard box or a grow tent over the printer does most of the job; the point is no draught
    • a filament dryer £30–90a food dehydrator with the trays out does the same job for less
  • Filled PLA wood, glow, metal Not as it stands

    Glow powder is the worst of them — it is effectively grit. Wood and metal fills also block a fine nozzle.

    Closest is your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, but it the fibre in it will eat a brass nozzle inside a spool, and it needs a 0.5 mm nozzle or bigger; this one is 0.4 mm.

    • a 0.5 mm hardened steel nozzle £8–20brass wears open as you print, so the holes creep wider and the part stops matching the model; and anything narrower bridges across the opening and blocks the melt zone
  • PVA dissolvable support Not as it stands

    Needs a second extruder to be worth anything, and it is even thirstier than nylon.

    Closest is your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, but it has to go on dry, and it is wet within a day of opening the bag.

    • a filament dryer £30–90a food dehydrator with the trays out does the same job for less
  • PEEK PEI, ULTEM Not as it stands

    Wants a 400 °C hotend and a chamber held near 120 °C. Listed so the answer is a clear no rather than a maybe.

    Closest is your Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, but it needs about 380 °C at the nozzle; this one stops at 300 °C, and it wants a 130 °C bed; this one stops at 110 °C, and it warps off an open bed — it needs still, warm air around it, and it has to go on dry, and it is wet within a day of opening the bag.

    • a high-temperature hotend rated past 380 °C £300+this is industrial-machine territory rather than a part swap
    • a bed that reaches 130 °C £30–70usually a mains-voltage heater and an SSR rather than a new printer
    • an enclosure £0–150a cardboard box or a grow tent over the printer does most of the job; the point is no draught
    • a filament dryer £30–90a food dehydrator with the trays out does the same job for less
