AlphaFold2 is measurably worse at predicting viral proteins.

0.927viral, median TM-score
0.970human cellular
p = 1.3 × 10⁻⁸233 solved structures

I compared AlphaFold2's predictions against experimentally solved crystal structures for 114 viral and 119 human proteins. A TM-score of 1.0 is a perfect match.

01

I found a bug that should have erased the result. It didn't.

My downloader took the first model the database returned. For poliovirus, that was a 22-residue fragment scored against a 283-residue protein. 33 proteins were affected, all viral. I fixed it and rebuilt the dataset three times. The gap held each time, on more data, with a smaller p-value.

02

It isn't missing alignments. Alignments are what partly rescue it.

My mentor suggested ESMFold, which predicts with no alignment step at all. On the same proteins the viral gap more than doubled, and AlphaFold2's edge over it was five times larger on viral proteins (p = 0.0014).

03

Viral origin and disorder are two separate blind spots.

Floppy, disordered proteins are hard for every model, and the penalty was identical under both programs. Being viral is a different penalty, and it doubled without alignments. Disorder is a property of the protein; viral origin is a gap in what the models learned from.

Method and limits
  1. Viral and human structures from RCSB, filtered by resolution, deduplicated by UniProt accession, balanced across four disorder levels.
  2. A protein enters only if an AlphaFold model covers its crystallised sequence. Final run: 4 mismatches of 250, down from 44.
  3. TM-align superposition; TM-score, RMSD, pLDDT, predicted disorder and coverage per protein and for 49,283 residues.
  4. Mann-Whitney and Welch tests agree; regression tm ~ disorder + coverage + is_viral with all VIFs near 1.
  5. Reproduced on a separate machine from a clean clone: every headline number matched and the figures came out byte-identical.
  • Proteins are taken in database order: reproducible, not random.
  • ESMFold's language model was also trained on databases where viruses are rare, so this is evidence for the explanation, not proof.
